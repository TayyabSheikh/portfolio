Academic Spotlight

Master's Thesis Research

M.Sc. Thesis Project Submitted

Evaluating Hyper-spherical Embeddings and Targeted Augmentations for Medical Image Analysis

Otto-Friedrich-Universität Bamberg Bamberg, Germany • Chair of Explainable Machine Learning

Abstract: Medical image classification faces challenges including limited labeled data, class imbalance, and domain shift across clinical sites. This thesis investigates whether projecting learned representations onto hyper-spherical manifolds (Sd-1), combined with domain-specific augmentations, can improve classification robustness. Evaluated on WILDS Camelyon17 (histopathology, inter-hospital variability) and Fitzpatrick17k (dermatology, skin tone bias), using MedMNIST-C corruptions for targeted augmentation design. The proposed MedAugmix strategy achieved 0.913 OOD accuracy on Camelyon17, a +6.0pp improvement over the ERM baseline (0.853), consistently outperforming all specialized HYPO configurations.

Hyper-spherical Spaces

Projected CNN features onto Sd-1 via contrastive objectives to enforce angular separation between classes, improving intra-class compactness and inter-class distance.

MedAugmix Strategy

Developed augmentation strategies using MedMNIST-C corruptions that simulate clinically relevant variations (stain shifts, morphological transforms) to improve performance on under-represented classes.

Clinical Benchmarks

Benchmarked on Camelyon17 (histopathology) and Fitzpatrick17k (dermatology) with evaluation of OOD accuracy, calibration, and robustness to distribution shift.
